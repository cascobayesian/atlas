Countries with voting blocs

Each country’s blocs come from the same model as the US atlas: a mixture model of how small areas (polling divisions, municipalities, communes, settlements) voted across several elections, with the number of blocs, K, chosen from the fits. Most use a Dirichlet-multinomial mixture of party vote counts; Switzerland, which votes on referendums, uses the beta-binomial mixture of yes and no votes. Pick a country for its bloc map at the chosen K and, where there is one, the principal-coordinates view of its areas.

    Where the records would let us look

    Finding voting blocs needs results for small areas, over several elections, in a form a computer can read, with boundaries to map them. Our regional surveys of election data (Africa, Asia, Europe, the Americas, Western Asia, the Caribbean and Oceania) graded each country’s records from A to F; the globe shows those grades in four steps.

    Orthographic projection: the globe as seen from space, half of it at a time. Boundaries: Natural Earth 1:50m (countries) and the US Census Bureau via us-atlas (states). Record grades from the regional surveys in Teaching/Country.Politics/Election.Data.Surveys, mapped A → excellent, B → good, C → limited, D and F → poor.
